response ----------------------> Concise tool assessment

Recommended toolset

I'd evolve your five tools into roughly 9–11 focused capabilities:

get_infrastructure_resources(infraId, credentials, type)

Inventory and ownership/topology.

get_resource_telemetry(resourceIds, timeRange)

CPU, memory, network, I/O, container and database metrics.

get_cost_details(infraId, resourceIds, timeRange)

Spend, forecast, unit economics, commitment utilization and allocation.

get_slo_details(serviceIds, timeRange)

Availability, latency, error rate, error budget and SLO targets.

get_scaling_and_capacity_details(resourceIds, timeRange)

Autoscaling configuration, traffic, capacity, saturation and historical scaling behavior.

analyze_optimization_opportunities(...)

Produces opportunities such as rightsizing, idle-resource removal, scheduling, storage lifecycle, DB tuning and commitment optimization.

create_change_plan(opportunities, constraints, userComments)

Converts recommendations into concrete proposed changes while respecting business/SLO constraints.

validate_change_plan(changePlan)

Checks policy, dependencies, blast radius, estimated savings, performance impact and resilience risk.

generate_iac_change(changePlan)

Generates Terraform/CloudFormation/Kubernetes/etc. changes.

create_change_pr(change)

Creates the PR for platform-team approval.
verify_change(changeId, validationWindow)
Measures whether the change actually improved cost/performance without violating SLOs.
What I'd change in your existing design

Your current:

get_infrasture_resources → get_resource_details → create_resource_details → user_comment_based_modification → modify_resource_details

is a reasonable CRUD/change-management foundation, but it doesn't yet represent an optimization agent.

The biggest conceptual improvement is to introduce this separation:

Observe → Analyze → Recommend → Plan → Validate → PR → Approve → Verify

rather than allowing user_comment_based_modification to directly drive infrastructure changes.

Bottom line

Your planned tools: ~65–70% fit for the stated use case.

They are particularly good for resource discovery, utilization retrieval, user-guided modification, and PR-based changes. The major missing pieces are FinOps data, SLO awareness, scaling/capacity analysis, optimization reasoning, safety validation, and post-change verification.

For this use case, I would not make modify_resource_details() directly perform an infrastructure modification. Make it a PR-generating operation after an explicit optimization/validation pipeline. That gives you the approval-based automation and resilience guarantees the scenario calls for.
